Minnesota Licensing at a Glance

Minnesota gives a permit at 15 and a license at 16 after driver ed. The 40-question test and road test are required.

What Makes Minnesota Different

Minnesota's manual adds snow-rated driving, lake-effect ice, many roundabouts, and transparent vs opaque ice.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 50 practice questions follow the topics in the official Minnesota driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

Minnesota Test-Day Tip

Minnesota applicants should review transparent vs opaque ice and the many roundabouts, frequent exam items.

Most-Missed Minnesota Topic

Verified Minnesota licensing and road-rule facts

These Minnesota-specific notes link back to the agency or handbook page used to verify them. Official requirements can change, so follow the linked source when preparing an application or test appointment.

  • Under ideal conditions and unless posted otherwise, Minnesota's handbook gives 10 mph in alleys, 30 mph on urban or town roads, and 55 mph on other roads. Official source ↗
  • Minnesota's standard DWI alcohol-concentration threshold is 0.08, while the threshold for operating a commercial vehicle is 0.04. Official source ↗
  • When a school bus displays red lights and an extended stop arm, drivers must stop at least 20 feet away; the handbook states a minimum $500 fine for a violation. Official source ↗

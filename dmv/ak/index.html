Alaska Licensing at a Glance

Alaska issues a learner permit at 14, among the youngest minimum ages in the nation, then a provisional at 16 and a full license at 16 and a half.

What Makes Alaska Different

Because many communities sit off the road network, the handbook stresses winter survival, wildlife on roads, and glare ice.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 50 practice questions follow the topics in the official Alaska driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

Alaska Test-Day Tip

On test day bring proof of identity and residency, and expect winter-driving questions; read each item fully before answering.

Most-Missed Alaska Topic

The most-missed Alaska item is glare-ice braking distance, so review the winter section until the longer stopping distances are automatic in your answers.

Key Alaska Road Rules

  • Yielding to moose and caribou on rural highways
  • Using headlights in the long twilight season
  • Chaining tires on mountain passes when posted

Safe Habits for AK

  • Keeping an emergency kit for cold remote drives
  • Telling someone your route on long hauls
  • Checking tires before snowy passes

Preparing for the AK Exam

Before your Alaska exam, set aside a week to read the manual and run our drills every evening; the short 20-question test rewards calm, careful reading more than speed.

On the Day in AK

On the day, arrive early with proof of identity and residency, take the full time the system allows, and re-read any sign question where two shapes look alike at a glance.

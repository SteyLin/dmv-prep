Idaho Licensing at a Glance

Idaho gives a permit at 14 and a half and a license at 16. The 40-question test and road test are required, with supervised driving logged.

What Makes Idaho Different

Idaho's handbook stresses mountain passes, wildlife crossings, and snow-rated tires, distinct from desert-state manuals.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 25 practice questions follow the topics in the official Idaho driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

Idaho Test-Day Tip

Idaho testers should study mountain-pass and wildlife-crossing rules, which appear more than in plains states.

Most-Missed Idaho Topic

The Idaho miss is usually the wildlife-crossing reduced speed zone, which the manual states with a specific number.

Preparing for the ID Exam

Study mountain-pass and wildlife-crossing rules closely, because Idaho weighs them more than plains states on the 40-question test.

On the Day in ID

Use snow-rated tires by season, slow in elk and deer zones, and yield at rural train crossings; the road test checks the same awareness.

After You Pass in ID

After the permit, log practice and book the road test; Idaho re-checks wildlife and mountain habits behind the wheel.

Use snow tires by season and carry the permit on every practice drive until the license arrives.

Verified Idaho licensing and road-rule facts

California Licensing at a Glance

California uses a three-step graduated system: permit at 15 and a half, provisional at 16, full at 17 and a half. The written test has 36 questions.

What Makes California Different

A required driver education course comes first, and the handbook devotes sections to lane splitting, carpool lanes, and mountain roads.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 58 practice questions follow the topics in the official California driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

California Test-Day Tip

California candidates should practise lane-splitting and carpool-lane rules separately, since they appear on both exams.

Most-Missed California Topic

The classic California miss is the lane-splitting speed differential, where the safe limit is stated specifically and tested by number.

How to Prepare for California

  • The lane-splitting speed differential

Key California Road Rules

  • Yielding to motorcycles in carpool lanes
  • Slowing in mountain curves by the posted limit
  • Observing the provisional passenger limits at 16

Safe Habits for CA

Preparing for the CA Exam

Work the California manual section by section, then drill our 36 questions daily; the lane-splitting and carpool rules need separate practice because they are unique to the state.

On the Day in CA

Book the behind-the-wheel exam only after driver education is done, and on test day watch the provisional passenger limits if you are still 16.

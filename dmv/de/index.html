Delaware Licensing at a Glance

Delaware gives a permit at 16 and a license at 16 and a half. The 20-question knowledge exam is shorter than most states.

What Makes Delaware Different

Delaware requires a vision test at every renewal and highlights small-state lane merges and beach-season traffic.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 50 practice questions follow the topics in the official Delaware driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

Delaware Test-Day Tip

Delaware testers should not rush the short 20-question exam; sign-shape wording differences are the usual miss.

Most-Missed Delaware Topic

Delaware testers often misread sign-shape questions where two shapes look similar at speed; learn shapes before words.

How to Prepare for Delaware

  • Sign shapes before the words
  • The road test after the written pass
  • Vision-corrected lenses if needed

Key Delaware Road Rules

  • Merging carefully in beach-season congestion
  • Yielding at small-intersection four-ways
  • Obeying the slower beach-zone limits

Preparing for the DE Exam

Learn sign shapes before the words, because Delaware's short 20-question exam trips people on similar-looking signs at speed rather than on meaning.

On the Day in DE

Bring vision-corrected lenses if you need them, merge early in beach-season traffic, and read every option before choosing on the short test.

After You Pass in DE

After the written pass, book the road test; Delaware re-checks sign recognition and beach-season merging behind the wheel.

Bring vision-corrected lenses if required, and keep the permit on every practice drive until the card comes.

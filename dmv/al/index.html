Alabama Licensing at a Glance

Alabama requires a learner license at 15 and a road test at 16. The knowledge exam has 30 questions from the state manual.

What Makes Alabama Different

ALEA runs licensing rather than a separate DMV, and the manual adds farm-vehicle and rural-road guidance that big-city manuals skip.

The Written Test on This Page

Our 51 practice questions follow the topics in the official Alabama driver handbook: road signs, right-of-way, speed limits, work zones, and sharing the road with cyclists, pedestrians, and large trucks. Drill them, then read every explanation to learn the rule, not just the answer.

Alabama Test-Day Tip

Alabama testers often miss farm-vehicle and rural-intersection rules, so review those chapters before booking.

Most-Missed Alabama Topic

A frequent Alabama miss is the right-of-way at uncontrolled rural intersections, where the manual rule differs from city four-way-stop habits.

Preparing for the AL Exam

Study the Alabama manual in two passes: first the general rules, then the farm-vehicle and rural sections that the 30-question exam weights more than drivers expect.

On the Day in AL

At the ALEA office, bring every document on the checklist, answer the 30 questions without rushing, and trust the study you did on uncontrolled-intersection yields.

California Driver License Guide

The California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) handles licenses for the nation's largest driving population. This guide covers the key steps for a first license, a Real ID, and a behind-the-wheel test.

Real ID Requirements

A Real ID compliant license is required for domestic flights and federal buildings. To get one, bring a passport or birth certificate, proof of Social Security number, and two proofs of California residency such as a lease and a utility bill.

Instruction Permit

Most applicants start with an instruction permit after passing the written knowledge exam. Permit holders under 18 must complete driver education and hold the permit for at least six months with 50 hours of supervised driving before the road test.

Behind-the-Wheel Test

The driving test checks lane changes, turns, parking, and observation. Practice in the test area beforehand — examiners look for smooth, legal, and confident control. Bring a currently registered and insured vehicle.

Teen vs. Adult Licensing

  • Under 18: permit + driver ed + 6-month wait + road test = provisional license
  • 18 and older: permit, then road test; no 6-month wait required
  • Provisional license restrictions lift at age 18

Official: dmv.ca.gov

Tip: Appointments are strongly recommended at most CA offices. Walk-in wait times can be very long.

About the California Test

California's knowledge exam draws from the state driver handbook and covers signs, laws, and safe driving. The test is administered at DMV offices, and a passing score is required before you can schedule the behind-the-wheel exam.

What to Study

  • Right-of-way at intersections and roundabouts
  • Speed limits and school zones
  • DUI laws and distracted-driving rules
  • Sharing the road with cyclists and pedestrians

Testing in California

First-time applicants take the written test at a DMV field office, often by appointment. Practice with our questions to build confidence, then review the handbook sections where you miss answers. Our material is study aid only and not affiliated with the DMV.

Permit to License Path

After the written test, California issues a permit, then a behind-the-wheel test once you meet requirements. Minors follow a graduated timeline with practice hours. Adults can often move faster, but the knowledge test still comes first.

California-Specific Rules

  • Right turn on red is allowed unless posted
  • Hand-held phone use is banned for all drivers
  • Headlights on in rain or snow

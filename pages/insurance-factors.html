Insurance

What Affects Your Rate

Insurers use dozens of variables to price a policy. Knowing the biggest levers helps you find savings and avoid surprises.

Top Factors

  • Driving record — tickets and at-fault crashes raise rates most
  • Age and experience — young and new drivers pay more
  • Location — city garaging costs more than rural
  • Vehicle — repair cost, theft rate, and safety ratings matter
  • Credit-based insurance score — a strong factor in most states
  • Annual mileage — more driving means more risk

What You Can Control

You cannot change your age, but you can keep a clean record, raise your deductible, lower your mileage, improve your credit score, and shop quotes every year. Vehicle choice at purchase time also locks in years of premium differences.

Tip: A sports car and a family sedan with the same driver can differ by thousands per year.

The Rating Factors

Premiums combine dozens of signals: your age, location, annual mileage, credit-based insurance score, vehicle type, and prior claims. No single factor decides the price, but a few heavy ones — like a sports car in a dense city — dominate.

What You Control

  • Your driving record and claims history
  • The car you choose to buy
  • Coverage limits and deductibles you pick
  • Where the car is parked overnight

What You Mostly Cannot

Age, gender in some states, and your city's theft rate are largely fixed. That is why shopping the same coverage across several insurers produces wide price gaps — each weights factors differently.

Credit and Insurance Score

In most states a credit-based insurance score influences the rate. Paying bills on time and lowering balances helps. It is not your credit report exactly, but responsible credit behavior generally lowers premiums.

Location Details

Even within a city, garaging ZIP and parking type change the price. A car in a garage in a low-theft area costs less than street parking in a dense one. When moving, re-quote — the address can swing the bill more than the car.

Bundling and Discounts

Combining auto with home or renters insurance often triggers a discount larger than either alone. Ask specifically about every available discount — good driver, paperless, multi-car — since they are not always applied by default.

Fuel Cost Calculator

Gas prices change often, and trip costs add up fast. This calculator estimates fuel needed and total cost from your distance, vehicle efficiency, and local price per gallon.

How to Use It

Enter your one-way or round-trip distance in miles, your vehicle's real-world MPG (check your last fill-up for accuracy), and the current price per gallon. The result shows gallons used and total dollars.

Why It Helps

  • Compare driving versus flying for a trip
  • Budget weekly commuting fuel
  • See how MPG improvements save money over a year

Quick Reference

A 300-mile trip at 28 MPG uses about 10.7 gallons. At $3.90 per gallon that is roughly $42 each way. Small MPG gains compound: a jump from 25 to 30 MPG cuts annual fuel cost by about 17 percent at the same mileage.

Tip: Use your actual MPG, not the window-sticker number — real driving is usually 10-20% lower.

How the Math Works

A fuel cost calculator multiplies distance by consumption and price: miles ÷ MPG × price per gallon. The result shows the true trip cost, not just the pump price. Two cars with a 5 MPG gap can differ by hundreds of dollars a year at normal mileage.

Comparing Vehicles

Enter the same annual miles for each candidate to see lifetime fuel differences. A car that costs more but sips fuel often wins over five years once fuel is counted, especially with high local prices.

Planning a Trip

Map the route distance and split by fuel stops so you carry enough budget. Knowing the per-tank range also tells you when to fill before rural stretches with no stations.

City vs. Highway

Real consumption is far worse in stop-and-go city driving than on open highway. Use a blended estimate for commute math, not the best-case highway number, or your budget will fall short each month.

Price Sensitivity

Small MPG gains matter more when fuel is expensive. At high local prices, a car that saves even 3 MPG can outweigh a cheaper purchase price within two or three years of normal driving.

Track Real World

Calculator estimates beat guesses, but your own log is best. Note each fill-up's miles and gallons; a drifting number reveals maintenance needs before the dashboard warns you.

SR-22 Insurance

An SR-22 is not an insurance policy — it is a form your insurer files with the state proving you carry the minimum required liability coverage. States require it for high-risk drivers after certain violations.

Who Needs an SR-22

  • Drivers convicted of DUI/DWI
  • Those caught driving without insurance
  • Drivers with repeated violations or a suspended license
  • Anyone the state orders to prove financial responsibility

How Long It Lasts

The filing period is usually three years, but it varies by state and offense. If your coverage lapses while the SR-22 is active, the insurer must notify the state, which can restart the clock or re-suspend your license.

Cost Impact

Filing the form itself costs a small fee, but the bigger effect is the higher premium that comes with a high-risk driver classification. Shopping around and keeping a clean record helps rates drop over time.

Tip: Keep continuous coverage for the full SR-22 period — a single lapse can undo your progress.

What an SR-22 Actually Is

An SR-22 is not insurance — it is a form your insurer files with the state proving you carry the required liability coverage. It is required after serious violations such as a DUI, driving without insurance, or repeated offenses.

How Long It Lasts

Most states require the filing for three years. If your coverage lapses during that time, the clock resets and the DMV is notified, which can re-suspend your license.

Cost Impact

The filing fee is small, but the underlying high-risk insurance is expensive. Shopping multiple insurers helps, and keeping a clean record after the period ends eventually lowers your rate back to normal.

Non-Owner SR-22

If you do not own a car but still need to prove coverage (for example, to reinstate a license), a non-owner policy with an SR-22 filing satisfies the requirement. It covers liability when you borrow or rent a vehicle.

After the Filing Ends

Once the period clears, tell your insurer to drop the filing. Keeping it active adds nothing but cost. Maintain continuous coverage afterward so your rate keeps falling back to standard levels.

Proof and Peace of Mind

Keep a copy of the filed SR-22 confirmation from your insurer. If the DMV questions your status, that document ends the dispute in minutes. Most insurers also list the filing on your declarations page for easy reference.

Points & Suspensions

Most states use a driver-violation point system. Each traffic conviction adds points to your record. Accumulating too many points within a set period can trigger a license suspension.

How Points Add Up

Minor moving violations such as speeding typically add 2 to 4 points. Serious offenses like reckless driving or leaving the scene add far more. Points usually expire after a few years of clean driving, but the conviction itself may stay longer.

Suspension Triggers

  • Reaching the state's point threshold (often 12 in a 12-month span)
  • DUI/DWI convictions
  • Driving without insurance or valid registration
  • Failure to appear in court or pay fines

Reinstatement

To lift a suspension you typically pay a reinstatement fee, serve the full suspension period, and provide proof of insurance (SR-22 in many cases). Completing a defensive-driving course may reduce active points in some states.

Tip: Check your state's point schedule after any ticket — a single serious offense can nearly max out your limit.

How Point Systems Work

Most states assign demerit points for moving violations. Minor speeding may add two or three points; reckless driving adds far more. Points stay on your record for a set period, often one to three years.

When a License Is Suspended

  • Too many points within a short window
  • A DUI or refusal to test
  • Unpaid tickets or failure to appear
  • Driving without insurance

Avoiding a Suspension

Attend traffic school where offered to mask points, pay citations on time, and contest errors promptly. A defensive-driving course can also lower insurance in some states. Driving on a suspended license turns a civil matter into a criminal one.

Checking Your Own Record

Most DMVs let you request a copy of your driving record online for a small fee. Reviewing it yearly catches errors — a ticket that was dismissed but still shows points, or a clerical mistake that could trigger a suspension.

Reinstating After Suspension

  • Pay the reinstatement fee
  • File proof of insurance (SR-22) if required
  • Complete any mandated course or hearing
  • Serve the full suspension period first

Out-of-State Tickets

Many states share violation data through interstate compacts. A speeding ticket in another state can still add points at home and trigger a suspension if ignored. Pay or contest it as if it happened locally.

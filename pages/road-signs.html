Road Signs Quick Reference

Knowing sign shapes and colors lets you react before you can read the words. This quick guide covers the categories tested on every written exam.

Sign Colors

  • Regulatory — white with black/red
  • Warning — yellow with black symbols
  • Guide — green with white text
  • Work zone / construction — orange

Shapes

  • Octagon = Stop
  • Triangle down = Yield
  • Circle = Railroad crossing
  • Diamond = Warning
  • Pentagon = School zone
  • Rectangle = Regulatory or guide

Regulatory Signs

Regulatory signs state the law: speed limits, no-turn, do-not-enter, and one-way streets. They are usually white with black lettering, or red for prohibitions. Disobeying them is a direct violation, so read them before you act.

Warning Signs

Warning signs describe road conditions ahead — curves, merges, deer crossings, slippery roads, or low bridges. They are yellow (or orange in work zones) with black symbols. Slow down and prepare when you see one.

Guide and Informational Signs

Green signs show destinations and distances; blue signs mark services like gas, food, and hospitals; brown signs point to recreation areas. These do not give orders but help you navigate.

Construction and Work Zones

Orange signs and flags mean road work. Fines often double in active work zones because crews are close to traffic. Merge early, follow flaggers, and never speed through.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing a yellow warning with a red regulatory sign
  • Missing a school-zone pentagon during reduced-speed hours
  • Not slowing for orange work-zone signs
  • Forgetting that a stop sign is always octagon-shaped, even at night
Tip: Learn shapes first, words second. Shape tells you the sign type before you can read it at speed.

Why Shapes Matter at Speed

At highway speed you see a sign's shape before its words. Training your eye to associate shape with meaning lets you react in the first moment. An octagon always means stop; a diamond always warns of a condition ahead.

Practice by Category

  • Regulatory: what you must or must not do
  • Warning: hazards and changing conditions
  • Guide: where you are and where to go
  • Work zone: crews and temporary changes

Test-Day Tip

Sign questions often ask the color or shape, not just the message. Drill the shape-to-meaning link until it is automatic, and the written exam's sign section becomes the easiest points on the page.

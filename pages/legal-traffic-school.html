Traffic School & Defensive Driving

Defensive-driving or traffic-school courses are state-approved programs that review safe-driving habits. Courts and DMVs sometimes allow them to offset a ticket or reduce points.

When It Helps

  • Masking or dismissing a minor moving violation (where allowed)
  • Reducing active points on your driving record
  • Lowering insurance premiums through a safe-driver discount

Eligibility Limits

States cap how often you can use traffic school for ticket dismissal, often once every 12 or 18 months. Serious offenses such as DUI usually do not qualify. A judge or DMV must approve the election in most cases.

What to Expect

Courses run in person or online, typically 4 to 8 hours, ending with a short exam. Upon completion, the provider files proof with the court or DMV. Keep your certificate as backup.

Tip: Elect traffic school before you pay the ticket — the option usually disappears once the fine is processed.

Why Take Traffic School

Completing an approved course can mask a violation's points, keep your insurance from rising, and in some cases satisfy a court order. It is usually offered for minor moving violations, not serious ones.

Online vs. Classroom

  • Online: study at your own pace, finish within the deadline
  • Classroom: fixed schedule, sometimes more engaging
  • Both must be state-approved to count

Important Limits

You can usually only use traffic school for point masking once within a set period (often 18 months). Using it does not erase the ticket from your record entirely — it hides the points from your insurance but the court record may remain.

Choosing a Provider

Only state-listed schools count. Scams advertise "instant" clearance that the DMV never receives. Verify the provider on your state's DMV website before paying, and keep the completion certificate until the court confirms receipt.

Deadline Discipline

The course must be finished and reported before the due date. A late completion usually means the points post to your record and the insurance increase sticks. Set a reminder a week early to absorb any technical delay.

When School Is Not Allowed

Serious charges — reckless driving, excessive speed, or a commercial license violation — usually disqualify you from point masking. In those cases only a clean record over time, or a successful court challenge, removes the points.

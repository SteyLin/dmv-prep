How to Parallel Park

Parallel parking is tested on many road exams and used daily in cities. The reference-point method below works in almost any car once you learn your own mirrors.

Positioning

Pull alongside the front car with about two feet of space, aligning your rear bumpers. Signal, check mirrors and blind spots, then shift to reverse.

Steering In

  • Turn the wheel fully toward the curb as you begin backing
  • When your front seat passes the other car's rear bumper, straighten briefly
  • When your front clears the car ahead, turn the wheel the other way to tuck in

Finishing

Center yourself in the space, leave room front and back, and shift to park. If crooked, pull forward and reverse once to adjust. Curb your wheels if local law requires.

Tip: Practice with cones in an empty lot before the test — muscle memory beats thinking under pressure.

Positioning

Pull alongside the front car with about two feet of space, aligned at its rear bumper. Shift to reverse, turn the wheel fully toward the curb, and back in slowly while watching the rear and the curb.

The Motion

  • Back in until your rear bumper clears the front car
  • Straighten, then cut away from the curb
  • Finish by easing toward the curb, centered

Practice and Judges

Most driving tests score this, so practice in empty lots first. Aim to be within a foot of the curb without touching it, and leave room for both neighboring cars to leave. Smooth, slow, and straight beats fast and crooked.

Curb and Clearance

Touching the curb gently is usually fine; mounting it or hitting a car is not. Keep speed low so corrections are small. Most testers care about control and safety, not a perfect line, so stay calm and deliberate.

Using References

Align your mirrors and rear window with the neighboring cars as visual marks. Fixed reference points beat guessing, and practice with cones builds the muscle memory that makes the real test automatic.

Reference Points Recap

Use the rear window alignment with the curb car as your cue to cut the wheel. Fixed points remove guesswork, and calm, slow movement beats rushing every time on test day.

License

Lost or Stolen License

If your license is lost, damaged, or stolen, you can request a replacement through your state DMV. A stolen license should also be reported to help prevent identity misuse.

If It Was Stolen

File a police report and keep the case number. While not always required for a replacement, it protects you if someone tries to use your identity. You can also place a fraud alert with the credit bureaus.

Replacement Steps

  • Gather proof of identity (passport, birth certificate, or prior ID)
  • Provide your Social Security number
  • Pay the duplicate card fee
  • Apply online, by mail, or in person depending on state rules

Temporary Driving

Many states issue a paper temporary license or receipt immediately, which is valid until the permanent card arrives by mail. Check whether your state requires a vision test or photo for duplicates.

Tip: Request the duplicate as soon as you notice the loss — driving without any valid ID can lead to a citation.

Act Quickly

Report a lost or stolen license to the DMV and, if stolen, to local police. A stolen license can be used for identity theft, so a police report helps if accounts are opened in your name.

Getting a Replacement

  • Apply online, by mail, or in person depending on your state
  • Provide your license number or alternate ID
  • Pay the duplicate fee
  • Receive a temporary paper license immediately

While You Wait

Carry the temporary license and a secondary photo ID. Some states issue a digital copy in their app. Avoid driving without any form of identification once the temp expires.

Identity Theft Follow-up

If your wallet with the license was stolen, monitor bank and credit accounts and consider a fraud alert with the credit bureaus. A thief with your license and a bill can attempt account takeovers, so speed matters more than the duplicate fee.

Out-of-State Replacements

Many states let residents replace a lost license online even while traveling, then mail the card to a home address. Carry a digital temp if your state app offers one so you are never driving without proof.

Temporary Validity

A paper temporary license is legally valid for driving until its printed expiry, but some bars and banks reject paper IDs. Keep a passport or other photo ID handy for age checks until the card arrives in the mail.

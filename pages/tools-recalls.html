NHTSA Recall Checker

Safety recalls are free fixes for defects that could cause crashes or injuries. Every vehicle should be checked regularly, especially before a long trip or a used-car purchase.

Check by VIN

The most accurate method is entering your 17-character VIN on the NHTSA recall lookup. This shows open recalls specific to your exact vehicle, even if the prior owner missed the notice.

Check by Make and Model

  • Enter year, make, and model for a general list of campaigns
  • Useful when shopping, to spot chronic problem years
  • Sign up for recall alerts by email where available

If There Is an Open Recall

Contact any authorized dealer for that brand — the repair is free regardless of where you bought the car or whether it is out of warranty. Unrepaired recalls can lower resale value and, more importantly, risk your safety.

Official: nhtsa.gov/recalls

Tip: Check recalls at every oil change — new campaigns are issued year-round.

Why Recalls Matter

A safety recall means the maker found a defect that can cause a crash or injury. Repairs are free at any franchised dealer, yet many cars on the road carry open recalls because owners never heard or forgot. An unfixed airbag or brake defect is a real daily risk.

How to Check

  • Enter your VIN on the NHTSA recall site
  • Check the manufacturer's owner page
  • Watch for mailed recall notices

After You Find One

Call a dealer with your VIN and book the repair. Parts for high-volume recalls can take months, so schedule early and keep driving cautiously until fixed. The repair does not cost you, only the wait does.

Used Car Recalls

A recall follows the vehicle, not the owner. A used car you buy may carry an open recall from years ago. Always check the VIN before purchase so you are not surprised by a safety defect after the sale closes.

Rental and Fleet Cars

Rental and commercial fleets must fix recalls, but a recently sold fleet car may have slipped. The same VIN check applies. A few minutes on the lookup site protects you from a known, fixable defect.

Stay Notified

Register your contact details with the manufacturer so future recalls reach you directly. Mailed notices get lost in moves, but an email or text alert follows you and keeps the car safe.

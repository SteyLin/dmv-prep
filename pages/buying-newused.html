Buying

New vs Used Car

The new-versus-used decision is mostly about total cost and risk. A new car costs more upfront but carries a full warranty and the latest safety tech. A used car costs less but may need earlier repairs.

Depreciation

A new car can lose 20 to 30 percent of its value in the first two years. Buying a one- or two-year-old used car lets someone else absorb that hit while you get most of the life left.

Warranty and Risk

  • New: full bumper-to-bumper coverage for 3 years, powertrain up to 5–10
  • Used: often sold as-is, or with a short dealer warranty
  • Certified pre-owned (CPO) adds a manufacturer-backed warranty at a premium

Which Fits You

If budget is tight and you keep cars long, a reliable used model is usually smarter. If you want predictable costs and the newest features, new may be worth the premium. Always budget for taxes, registration, and insurance on top of the price.

Tip: Run a total-cost-of-ownership estimate, not just the sticker price, before deciding.

New Car Pros and Cons

A new car brings a full warranty, the latest safety tech, and no hidden history. The downside is steep depreciation the moment you drive off — often 20% in year one. If you keep cars many years, that loss matters less.

Used Car Pros and Cons

A used car costs far less and skips the worst depreciation, but may carry wear, limited warranty, and unknown history. A certified pre-owned model splits the difference with an inspection and a short warranty at a mid price.

Making the Call

  • New: best if you keep it long and want warranty peace
  • Used: best if budget matters and you inspect well
  • CPO: best if you want savings with some backing

Match the choice to how long you keep cars and how much risk you accept, then negotiate the specific example on its condition.

Depreciation Reality

A new car can lose 20–30% of value in the first two years. A two-year-old used car lets someone else absorb that drop while still offering modern safety. If resale value matters, used often wins on total cost.

Warranty Thinking

New cars include a factory warranty that fades by year three. Used cars past it should be inspected harder. Budget a small repair fund either way so a surprise bill does not derail your budget.

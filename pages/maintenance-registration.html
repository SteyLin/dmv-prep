Title & Registration

A vehicle title proves ownership; registration lets you legally drive it. Both are handled by your state DMV or motor vehicle agency, and rules differ by state.

Title Transfer

When you buy or sell a car, the seller signs the title over to the buyer. The buyer then submits it to the state within a set window (often 10 to 30 days) and pays a transfer fee. Lienholders are listed until the loan is paid off.

Registration Steps

  • Provide the signed title and a bill of sale
  • Show proof of insurance and emissions/safety test if required
  • Pay registration and plate fees
  • Receive plates and a registration sticker or card

Renewal

Most registrations renew yearly or every two years. Many states allow online renewal; late renewal can add penalties and a citation risk.

Tip: Photograph the title signature area before mailing — lost titles create costly duplicate delays.

Renewal Deadlines

Most states mail a renewal notice weeks ahead, but the deadline is your responsibility even if the notice is lost. Set a calendar alert; driving with expired tags brings a citation and sometimes impound fees.

What You May Need

  • Proof of insurance on file
  • A passed emissions or safety test
  • Payment of any property tax due

Updating Records

Notify the DMV after a move or sale so notices reach you and the title stays clean. An unreported sale can leave you liable for the buyer's tolls and crashes until the transfer is recorded.

Plate and Sticker Rules

Some states require front and rear plates; others one. Place the validation sticker where the state specifies — a misplaced sticker can draw a stop. Replace a faded or stolen plate promptly to avoid being flagged.

Disabled or Military Holds

Active-duty members often get renewal extensions; check before letting a tag lapse while deployed. Some states waive fees for disabled veterans. Know your eligibility so you neither overpay nor accidentally expire.

Selling or Gifting a Car

File a release of liability the day you hand over the keys. It shifts future tolls, parking, and crash responsibility to the new owner. A bill of sale with date and odometer protects both sides if questions arise later.

New Car Buying Checklist

Buying a car is a major purchase. Walk through this checklist before you sign so you do not overlook costs or skip key checks that protect your money.

  • Set budget including tax, title, and insurance.
  • Compare models and check reliability ratings.
  • Test drive on highway and local roads.
  • Check incentive, rebate, and financing options.
  • Review out-the-door price before signing.
  • Confirm warranty terms and maintenance schedule.
Tip: Let the salesperson speak first. Price hidden until you’re ready to buy.

Before You Shop

Set a firm budget that includes tax, registration, insurance, and a safety inspection — not just the sticker price. Knowing your financing limit before you arrive prevents dealers from steering you toward payments you cannot afford.

Inspect and Verify

  • Check the VIN on the dashboard matches the title and door jamb
  • Review the vehicle history for accidents and title brands
  • Confirm open recalls on the manufacturer or NHTSA site
  • Test drive on both city streets and a highway

Closing the Deal

Read the full contract, not just the monthly payment line. Walk away if a fee appears that the salesperson cannot explain. A cooling-off period rarely applies to used cars, so the signature is final.

New vs. Used Trade-offs

A new car brings a full warranty and the latest safety tech but loses value the moment you drive off. A certified pre-owned car splits the difference with a limited warranty at a lower price. Match the choice to how long you plan to keep the vehicle.

Hidden Ownership Costs

  • Annual registration and property taxes
  • Insurance rated on the model's risk and repair cost
  • Fuel or charging, maintenance, and tires
  • Depreciation, the cost most buyers forget

Private Seller Caution

Buying from an individual skips dealer fees but offers no warranty. Meet in a public place, verify the title is in the seller's name, and run a bill-of-sale. If the seller hesitates on paperwork, walk away — the savings are not worth a branded title.

Final Walk-Around

Before handing over money, walk the car in daylight and check paint, glass, tires, and lights. A dent or mismatched panel can mean prior damage not in the history report. Note anything odd and deduct it from your offer or ask for repair.

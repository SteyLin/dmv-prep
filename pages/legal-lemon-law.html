Lemon Law Basics

Lemon laws protect buyers of new (and in some states used) vehicles that have recurring defects the dealer cannot fix after a reasonable number of attempts. Each state sets its own thresholds and deadlines.

Repair-Attempt Threshold

Most states presume a lemon after three or four repair attempts for the same serious defect, or if the vehicle is out of service for a cumulative total (often 30 days) within the warranty period. Minor issues usually do not qualify.

Refund or Replacement

If a vehicle is declared a lemon, the manufacturer must either refund the purchase price (minus a mileage allowance) or replace the vehicle with a comparable one. The choice often depends on what the law requires and what the buyer prefers.

Filing a Claim

  • Keep every repair order, invoice, and communication record
  • Notify the manufacturer in writing during the warranty period
  • Many states offer a free arbitration program before court
Tip: Start a repair log on day one. Missing documentation is the most common reason lemon claims fail.

What Qualifies as a Lemon

A lemon is a new or leased vehicle with a defect that substantially impairs use, value, or safety and cannot be fixed after a reasonable number of repair attempts. Each state defines "reasonable" differently — commonly three or four tries for the same problem, or 30 days out of service.

Document Everything

  • Keep every repair order with dates and mileage
  • Note the specific complaint each visit
  • Save written communications from the dealer

Filing a Claim

Most states require you to notify the manufacturer and attempt a final repair before arbitration. If the claim succeeds, you are entitled to a refund or replacement vehicle, and the manufacturer usually pays attorney fees.

Used Car Note

Standalone lemon laws mostly cover new vehicles. "As-is" used sales rarely qualify, so a pre-purchase inspection matters even more.

What the Manufacturer Owes

If your vehicle qualifies, the manufacturer must refund the purchase price (minus a mileage offset) or provide a comparable replacement. You generally keep the choice between refund and replacement in most states.

Time Limits

Lemon-law rights usually expire at the end of the warranty or a set time after delivery. Document problems early so you are inside the window when you file. Waiting until the warranty ends can forfeit the claim.
